 
H5. Frontline Health-Worker Companion for Low-Connectivity Areas 
Real-world pain: An ASHA or ANM worker visits villages with a phone, patchy 2G, and 
responsibility for maternal, child, and chronic-disease follow-ups across hundreds of 
households. She needs answers from a protocol book she cannot carry, in her own language, 
oflen with no signal at all. 
The problem: Build an assistant that answers frontline health-worker questions from oﬃcial 
protocol documents, works oﬄine or on very poor connectivity, and knows when to escalate to 
a doctor. 
What we provide: 
 
 A corpus of simpliﬁed public-health protocol documents: antenatal care schedule, 
immunisation schedule, danger signs in pregnancy and newborns, malnutrition screening, 
hypertension and diabetes follow-up, ORS and fever management 
 200 realistic worker questions in code-mixed language, with hidden expected answers and 
hidden "must escalate" ﬂags 
 A small oﬄine-capable language model and embedding model, pre-cached 
 A synthetic household register of 500 families with due-date and immunisation-due 
information 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 Device is a low-end Android phone; assume tight memory and no GPU 
 Connectivity may be zero for hours — the core path must work oﬄine 
 The worker is a trained paramedical professional, not a doctor, and not a layperson 
 No integration with any government system exists 
Desired output: 
 
1. A working question-answering ﬂow grounded strictly in the provided protocols, with the 
protocol section cited in every answer 
2. A demonstrated oﬄine mode, plus a measured footprint: model size, memory use, and 
response time on a CPU. This is scored — eﬃciency is a ﬁrst-class result here, not an 
aflerthought 
3. A hard-coded danger-sign escalation layer that ﬁres before any model reasoning ("if 
bleeding in third trimester → refer immediately"). evaluate. py checks 100% detection on 
the escalation set — anything less than perfect fails this criterion 
4. A daily visit list generated from the household register: who is due for what, prioritised 
5. Refusal behaviour on out-of-scope questions ("what dose of this antibiotic?") with a clear 
redirect to a doctor 
Stretch: voice input in one Indian language, and a sync-when-connected queue for collected 
data.
