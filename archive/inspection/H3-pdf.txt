 
H3. Predicting the 30-Day Readmission — And Doing Something About It 
Real-world pain: A meaningful share of discharged patients bounce back within 30 days. Many 
of those returns were preventable with a phone call, a medication check, or a follow-up 
appointment. But a hospital's care-coordination team can only reach a handful of patients a 
day. 
The problem: Predict 30-day readmission risk at the moment of discharge, and — more 
importantly — turn that risk into a ranked, resource-constrained intervention list. 
What we provide: 
 
 25k synthetic discharge records: demographics, primary and secondary diagnoses (coded), 
length of stay, prior admissions, procedures, discharge disposition, medication count, key 
lab values 
 Deliberate real-world messiness: ~15% missing labs, inconsistent coding, some duplicate 
patient records 
 Hidden 30-day readmission outcome 
 A stated constraint: the care team can call only 50 patients per week 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 Post-discharge behaviour (whether they ﬁlled the prescription, attended follow-up) is 
unobserved 
 Socioeconomic and distance-from-hospital data is unavailable 
 Some readmissions are planned/unavoidable — teams should reason about whether these 
should be excluded 
 Missingness may itself be informative (a lab not ordered tells you something) 
Desired output: 
 
1. A calibrated risk score per patient — calibration is explicitly scored, because a "70%" that 
means 30% is useless for prioritisation 
2. evaluate. py score = AUC-PR plus recall@50 (the metric that respects the real constraint), 
plus a calibration error term 
3. Per-patient drivers of risk, expressed as something a nurse can act on ("11 medications, no 
follow-up scheduled, third admission in 6 months") 
4. A bias audit across age, sex, and payer type — required, not optional. Report performance 
gaps and describe mitigation 
5. A one-page "Monday morning call list" for the care team, and a rough estimate of 
readmissions averted 
 
Stretch: recommend a speciﬁc intervention type per patient (pharmacist call vs. home visit vs. 
appointment booking) and justify the mapping.
