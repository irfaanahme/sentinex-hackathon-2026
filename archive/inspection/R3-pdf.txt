 
 
R3. The Conversational Shopping Assistant That Doesn't Lie 
Real-world pain: Retailers are rushing to put chatbots on product catalogues. Most of them 
conﬁdently invent products, prices, and return policies. A hallucinated price is a legal and trust 
problem, not a cosmetic one. 
The problem: Build a grounded product-discovery assistant over a ﬁxed catalogue that answers 
natural shopper questions and refuses to answer when the catalogue doesn't support it. 
What we provide: 
 
 A catalogue of 5,000 products: title, description, attributes, price, stock status, category, 
ratings 
 A policy document (returns, shipping, warranty) as plain text 
 150 test questions with hidden expected behaviour, deliberately including: answerable 
questions, ambiguous questions, out-of-catalogue questions ("do you sell iPhones?"), and 
trap questions ("what's the price of the blue running shoe?" when three exist) 
 A small embedding model and small instruct model, pre-cached for oﬄine use 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 Catalogue is the single source of truth; nothing outside it may be asserted 
 Prices and stock are current as of the snapshot 
 No user account, order history, or payment integration exists 
 Shoppers write short, ungrammatical, code-mixed queries 
Desired output: 
 
1. A working chat interface (notebook, CLI, or simple web page — all score equally) 
2. Every factual claim must carry a citation to a product ID or policy section 
 
3. An explicit refusal / clariﬁcation path — teams are scored on correctly saying "I don't have 
that" and on asking a disambiguating question rather than guessing 
4. evaluate. py score = groundedness (no unsupported claims), answer helpfulness, and 
refusal accuracy — with hallucinated prices carrying a heavy penalty 
5. A latency and token-cost report per query, since the budget is ﬁxed and equal 
Stretch: handle a multi-turn budget constraint ("under ₹2,000, and I already said I want 
cotton").
