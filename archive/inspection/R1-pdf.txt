R1. The Phantom Stock-Out Detector 
Real-world pain: A store's system says 6 units are in stock, so the item never gets reordered and 
never shows as out-of-stock online — but the units are actually lost, misplaced, or stolen. The 
shelf is empty, sales quietly go to zero, and nobody notices for weeks. This is one of retail's most 
expensive invisible leaks. 
The problem: Given daily sales and system inventory, ﬂag SKU–store combinations where the 
recorded stock is almost certainly wrong, and rank them so a store manager can physically 
verify only the top 20 each morning. 
What we provide: 
 
 18 months of daily sales for ~800 SKUs across 25 stores (synthetic, with realistic seasonality, 
promotions, and weekday patterns) 
 System-recorded on-hand inventory per SKU-store-day 
 A promotion calendar and a store master (size, format, city tier) 
 A hidden ground-truth label on ~3% of rows marking real phantom stock-outs (held back 
from teams; used by evaluate. py) 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 Sales of exactly zero for N consecutive days while stock is positive is suspicious — they 
choose N and justify it 
 
 Demand is roughly stable week-over-week unless a promotion or holiday is ﬂagged 
 Slow-moving SKUs legitimately have zero-sales days; the model must not drown in these 
false positives 
 No store-level camera, RFID, or staﬀdata exists 
 A manager can verify at most 20 SKUs per store per day — the model must respect this 
budget 
Desired output: 
 
1. A ranked daily "go check this shelf" list: store, SKU, conﬁdence, and a one-line reason in 
plain English 
2. evaluate. py score = precision@20 per store-day (the metric that actually matters to a 
manager), plus recall on the hidden labels 
3. A simple screen or notebook view showing the sales-vs-stock chart for any ﬂagged SKU so 
the manager trusts the alert 
4. An estimate of annual rupees recovered, with the arithmetic shown 
Stretch: distinguish "phantom stock-out" from "misplaced on wrong shelf" from "delisted item" 
using diﬀerent signal shapes.
