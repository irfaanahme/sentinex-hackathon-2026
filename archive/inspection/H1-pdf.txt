PART B — HEALTHCARE PROBLEM STATEMENTS 
 
 
H1. OPD Triage Assistant for an Overloaded Government Hospital 
Real-world pain: An outpatient department sees 800 walk-ins a day with two doctors. Patients 
are seen ﬁrst -come-ﬁrst-served, so a person with chest pain waits behind 40 people with mild 
fever. The triage decision today is made by an overworked nurse in 30 seconds. 
The problem: From a short intake form and a free-text complaint, assign a triage priority and 
recommend the right department — safely. 
What we provide: 
 
 30k synthetic intake records: age, sex, vitals (BP, pulse, temp, SpO2), free-text chief 
complaint, known conditions, medication list 
 Complaint text written the way patients actually speak, translated/transliterated across 
languages 
 A published 5-level triage scale with clinical deﬁnitions (an ESI-style rubric) in the domain 
primer 
 Hidden labels: correct triage level and correct department, plus a ﬂag for the ~4% of cases 
that are true emergencies 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 No labs, imaging, or ECG available at triage — only the intake form 
 Vitals may be missing or mis-entered (teams must handle 0 BP, temp of 96°C) 
 Patients may under-report or over-report symptoms 
 Nurse has ≤60 seconds per patient, so the interface must be near-instant 
Desired output: 
 
1. A triage level (1–5), recommended department, and the red-ﬂag symptoms detected 
2. Asymmetric safety is the core requirement: under-triaging a true emergency must be 
penalised ~20× more than over-triaging a mild case. evaluate. py implements this — teams 
that optimise plain accuracy will score badly, and discovering this is part of the learning 
3. A mandatory hard-coded escalation rule layer that overrides the model on critical vitals 
(teams must show these rules) 
4. Sensitivity/recall on the hidden true-emergency set, reported separately and prominently 
 
5. An abstention mechanism: the system may say "cannot assess — send to nurse now" and is 
rewarded for using it appropriately 
Stretch: estimate expected wait-time reduction versus the current ﬁrst-come-ﬁrst-served 
baseline using the provided arrival log.
