 
H2. Discharge Summary to Patient-Understandable Care Plan 
Real-world pain: A patient goes home with a two-page discharge summary full of 
abbreviations, dosages, and follow-up instructions they cannot read. Non-adherence and 
avoidable readmission follow. In India, add the layer that the patient may not read English. 
The problem: Convert a clinical discharge summary into a faithful, plain-language care plan 
the patient and their family can actually follow — without adding, dropping, or altering any 
medical instruction. 
What we provide: 
 
 2,000 synthetic discharge summaries across common conditions (diabetes, post-op 
recovery, cardiac, respiratory, maternity), with clinical abbreviations and dosage tables 
 A medical abbreviation glossary and a drug reference table (generic name, common brand, 
dose form) 
 50 gold-standard human-written plain-language versions as reference examples 
 An evaluation harness that checks whether every medication, dose, frequency, and follow-
up date from the source appears in the output 
Assumptions students may make: 
 
 The source summary is clinically correct and complete — nothing may be inferred beyond 
it 
 Target reading level: roughly Class 8 
 Assume no access to the patient's full EHR beyond the summary 
 Patient may have low literacy, so structure and simplicity matter more than prose quality 
Desired output: 
 
1. A structured care plan: medicines (what, how much, when, how long), warning signs that 
mean "return to hospital immediately", diet and activity guidance, and follow-up 
appointments 
2. Faithfulness is scored above ﬂuency. evaluate. py runs an extraction check: any dropped 
medication, altered dose, or invented instruction is a hard penalty 
3. Output in English plus one Indian language ofthe team's choice, with the same 
faithfulness check applied to the translation 
4. A readability score on the output, reported 
5. A one-page "fridge sheet" version — the actual artefact a family would use 
 
Stretch: generate a spoken audio version for a patient who cannot read, and a 7-day medicine 
timetable.
